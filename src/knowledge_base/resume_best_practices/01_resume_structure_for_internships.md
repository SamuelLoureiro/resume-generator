# Ideal Resume Structure for Internship Applications

## 1. The Golden Rule: Exactly 1 Page
For internship and entry-level candidates, the resume must be **strictly one page**. Recruiters and technical managers spend an average of 6 to 15 seconds on the initial screening of each resume. A two-page resume for someone who has not yet graduated signals a lack of conciseness and poor synthesis skills.

## 2. Strategic Section Hierarchy for Students
The order of sections should reflect the student's current career stage:

1. **Header:**
   - Full name with prominent typographic weight.
   - Essential contact details: City/State, phone number (with area code), professional email address.
   - Direct, clickable links: updated LinkedIn, active GitHub, and portfolio (if available).
   - **What to omit:** Photo, government ID numbers (SSN/national ID), marital status, full street address.

2. **Professional Objective (Tailored Objective):**
   - 2 to 3 lines extremely targeted to the specific position.
   - Must mention the desired role and how the candidate's competencies add value to the company's area.
   - Avoid generic phrases such as "seeking an opportunity to grow within the company."

3. **Academic Education:**
   - For students, education must appear at the top (right after the objective), as it is the primary eligibility requirement for internships.
   - Include: Degree name (Bachelor's/Associate's), institution name, start date, and expected graduation date (month/year).
   - Relevant highlights: undergraduate research, teaching assistantship, high GPA (if applicable), exchange programs, or elective courses aligned with the position.

4. **Experience (Professional, Academic, or Extracurricular):**
   - For internships, any hands-on experience counts: previous internships, funded undergraduate research, junior enterprise involvement, teaching assistantships, or research labs.
   - Reverse chronological order (most recent first).
   - Each role must include: job title, organization/company, period, and location.
   - 2 to 4 bullet points focused on achievements and impact — never passive descriptions of daily tasks.

5. **Featured Projects (Portfolio Projects):**
   - Extremely high-weight section for technology and data positions.
   - Highlight 2 to 3 practical, functional projects using the tech stack requested in the job posting.
   - Include repository/demo link, technologies used, and bullet points explaining the challenge and the outcome.

6. **Technical Skills:**
   - Categorized by domain: Programming Languages, Frameworks/Libraries, Databases, Tools/DevOps.
   - Enables fast scanning by ATS robots and technical recruiters.

7. **Languages and Certifications:**
   - Languages with clear proficiency level (e.g., English — Advanced/C1 or Intermediate).
   - Certifications with a recognized issuing body and year.

## 3. Visual Scannability Guidelines
- Balanced margins (between 1.2 cm and 1.5 cm).
- Clean typography with consistent vertical spacing.
- Use bold only for anchor information (job titles, project names, key metrics).
- Adequate spacing between sections with subtle dividing lines.
